Day 1, Morning • All Domains

The CISSP Exam and the Security Leader Mindset

CISSP is not a technical exam with management questions. It is a management exam with technical answers. Before the first domain, learn how the exam is built, how it decides, what the ISC2 Code of Ethics asks of you, and the one habit that turns hard questions into easy ones: think like the person accountable for the outcome.

Session: Day 1, 9:00 to 10:30 Duration: ~1.5 hrs Exam Weight: All domains Knowledge Check: 8 Questions

Overview

Know the exam before you study for it

The Certified Information Systems Security Professional credential is built for people who design, lead, and govern security programs. The exam covers eight domains, from risk management to software security. Breadth matters more than depth. A candidate who knows a little about everything, and knows how a leader would decide, does better than a specialist who knows one area deeply.

This orientation covers eligibility, the eight domains and their weights, how the adaptive exam works, the ISC2 Code of Ethics, the manager mindset, and what happens after you pass. It also sets up the five days of this workshop.

🧭 Think like a managerOn most CISSP questions, several answers would work. Your job is to pick the one a senior security leader would choose: protect people first, follow governance and policy, involve the owner, and advise rather than fix it yourself.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to…

  • Describe the CISSP eligibility rules, the Associate of ISC2 path, and the endorsement process. Bloom: Understand
  • Compare the eight domains and their exam weights, and plan study time to match. Bloom: Analyze
  • Explain how computerized adaptive testing selects items and decides when to stop. Bloom: Understand
  • Apply the four canons of the ISC2 Code of Ethics, in order, to a conflict. Bloom: Apply
  • Choose the BEST, FIRST, or MOST answer using the security leader mindset. Bloom: Evaluate
  • Plan a personal study schedule that matches effort to domain weight. Bloom: Create

What CISSP Is and Who It Is For

CISSP is a vendor-neutral credential from ISC2 for experienced security practitioners, managers, and architects. It signals that the holder understands security across the whole enterprise and can lead a program, not only operate a tool. For faculty, it adds a recognized credential to your teaching record and a framework that connects every security course you teach.

Eligibility

  • Experience: five years of cumulative, paid, full-time work in two or more of the eight domains. Part-time work and internships can count toward the total under ISC2’s rules.
  • Waiver: a four-year college degree (or regional equivalent), or an additional credential from ISC2’s approved list, can satisfy one year. Only one year can be waived in total.
  • No experience yet: you can still sit the exam. Passing makes you an Associate of ISC2, and you then have six years to earn the five years of experience.
💡 Faculty noteTeaching security courses can count as experience when the work covers the domains. Keep a record of the courses, the domains each one covers, and your dates. You will need it for endorsement.

The Eight Domains

The exam outline divides the body of knowledge into eight domains. The weights tell you where questions come from, and they should shape where you spend study time. This workshop follows the ISC2 CISSP exam outline effective April 15, 2027. Exams taken before that date used the previous outline from 2024, so older study guides may show different weights and a different name for Domain 1.

The weights are flat. The heaviest domains, Security Governance and Risk Management and Identity and Access Management, carry 13.6% each. The lightest, Security Assessment and Testing and Software Development Security, still carry 11.2%. That spread is under two and a half points, so no domain can be skipped and none deserves twice the time of another.

Plan study time by weakness, not by weight. A domain you already know well needs review, and a domain you rarely work in needs real hours. The adaptive exam samples every domain, and a weak one can sink an otherwise strong candidate.

💡 Exam TipDomain 1 is named Security Governance and Risk Management. Its first two objectives anchor the whole exam: follow the ISC2 Code of Professional Ethics and your organization’s code, and apply the five security concepts of confidentiality, integrity, availability, authenticity, and non-repudiation.
3

How the Adaptive Exam Works

The English CISSP exam uses computerized adaptive testing. Instead of giving everyone the same fixed form, the exam estimates your ability after each answer and chooses the next item to match it. Answer correctly and the next item tends to be harder. Miss one and it tends to be easier.

FeatureWhat to expect
Length100 to 150 items, including unscored pretest items you cannot identify.
TimeUp to 3 hours.
Passing700 out of 1000 on a scaled score.
Item typesMultiple choice plus advanced types such as drag-and-drop and hotspot.
NavigationOne item at a time. You cannot skip, go back, or change an earlier answer.
StoppingThe exam ends when it is confident you are above or below the passing standard, when you reach the maximum number of items, or when time runs out.

Two consequences surprise most candidates. First, because items are pitched near your ability, nearly everyone feels they are missing a lot of questions. That feeling is the design working, not a sign of failure. Second, the length of your exam says nothing on its own. A short exam can be a clear pass or a clear fail.

💡 Exam TipBudget about 70 seconds per item. At that pace, 150 items fit in 3 hours with a small margin. Do not spend five minutes on one question you cannot take back anyway.

🔎 In practice

Three lessons fall out of the model. The percent correct hovers near 50% for every candidate, because the items keep moving to meet them. Strong and weak candidates both finish early, since their bands clear the line quickly. Candidates near the standard run long, so a 150-item exam is a close call either way. In the model, as in the real exam, the clock matters most for slow, borderline candidates.

The ISC2 Code of Ethics

Every ISC2 member agrees to the Code of Ethics, and the exam tests it directly in Domain 1. The preamble sets the frame: the safety and welfare of society and the common good come first, along with duty to principals and to each other, and the code asks members to hold to the highest ethical standards. Four canons follow, and their order is a priority order.

CanonThe duty, in plain wordsExample
1. SocietyProtect society, the common good, public trust and confidence, and the infrastructure.Report a flaw that endangers public safety, even when it is inconvenient for your employer.
2. HonorAct honorably, honestly, justly, responsibly, and legally.Do not overstate your findings or claim credit for someone else’s work.
3. PrincipalsProvide diligent and competent service to your employer or client.Deliver the assessment you were hired for, on time, within your competence.
4. ProfessionAdvance and protect the profession.Mentor newcomers and do not bring discredit to the field.

Read the official wording at isc2.org/ethics. Most employers also have an organizational code of ethics. A CISSP follows both, and when they conflict, the ISC2 canons and the law set the floor.

💡 Exam TipWhen an ethics question pits an employer’s request against public safety or the law, the earlier canon wins. Protecting society comes before serving a principal. Also remember that only members can be sanctioned under the code, and ISC2 limits who may file a complaint under certain canons.

Ethics and artificial intelligence

The outline lists ethics in artificial intelligence under the legal and regulatory objective. Apply the canons to AI decisions the same way you apply them to anything else. Training a model on personal data without a lawful basis fails the duty to act legally. An automated decision that denies people a service, with no way to explain or appeal it, fails the duty to act honorably and responsibly. A CISSP asks who is accountable for the model, what data it uses, and how its results are checked.

6

The Security Leader Mindset

Most failed attempts come from candidates who answer as engineers. The exam rewards the answer a senior leader would give. These filters help you choose among several plausible options.

  • People first: human safety outranks data, systems, and money. If a fire breaks out during an incident, evacuate first.
  • Governance before technology: understand the business need, the policy, and the owner before you choose a control.
  • Advise, do not fix: you recommend and senior management decides. The owner accepts risk, not the security team.
  • Process over heroics: follow the incident, change, and evidence procedures. Undocumented quick fixes are rarely correct.
  • Root cause over symptom: prefer answers that fix why something happened, not only what happened.

Reading the qualifier

BEST or MOSTSeveral answers may help. Pick the one with the widest benefit and the strongest alignment with governance, often the one that addresses the cause or protects the most.
FIRSTSeveral answers may be correct steps. Pick the one that comes first in a sound process. Assess before you act, and contain before you eradicate.
💡 Exam TipRead the last sentence of the question first. It tells you what is being asked and from which role. Then read the scenario for the facts that matter.

After the Exam and Your Study Plan

Endorsement, CPEs, and costs

  • Endorsement: after passing, submit your endorsement application within nine months. An active ISC2 credential holder attests to your experience, or ISC2 can act as endorser.
  • CPE credits: 120 CPEs per three-year cycle. Aim for about 40 a year so the last year is not a scramble. Teaching, attending conferences, and writing can all count.
  • Fees: the exam is about $749 USD and the annual maintenance fee is about $135. These figures are approximate; confirm current pricing at isc2.org before budgeting.

8

Faculty Teaching Segment

The mindset in this lesson is teachable. Students who learn to ask “who owns this decision?” write better answers in every course you teach.

  • Signature assignment: students receive three short scenarios in which an employer’s request conflicts with a canon. For each, they name the canon in play, rank the duties, and write a two-sentence recommendation to a supervisor.
  • Rubric criteria: correct canon identified, correct priority order applied, a recommendation that respects the law and the owner’s authority, and a professional tone.
  • Activity idea: run the CAT Explainer on a projector. Ask students to predict the length of an exam for three abilities before you run 25 exams for each. It defuses test anxiety for any adaptive exam they will take.
  • Course fit: the ethics and mindset content supports an introductory cybersecurity course and the CAE-CD Cybersecurity Fundamentals, Cybersecurity Principles, and Policy, Legal, Ethics, and Compliance Knowledge Units.
🎓 Teaching noteAsk participants to share one decision from their own career that they would now answer differently with the manager mindset. Those stories make the filters memorable for the rest of the week.

Scored Knowledge Check

Test your understanding

Select the best answer for each question, then submit for your score and the rationale for every item. Like the exam, several options may be true; choose the best one.

Q1.A CISSP’s employer asks them to stay quiet about a control system flaw that could endanger the public. Which canon takes priority?

Question 1 options
Correct: B. The canons are ordered by priority. Protecting society is the first canon and outranks the duty to a principal. The right path is to raise the issue through proper channels and, if needed, the appropriate authorities.

Q2.A candidate’s CISSP exam ends after exactly 100 items. What can the candidate conclude?

Question 2 options
Correct: D. An adaptive exam stops early when the ability estimate is clearly above or clearly below the standard. Short exams happen to both strong and weak candidates, so length alone tells you nothing.

Q3.A faculty member has three years of qualifying security experience and a bachelor’s degree. They pass the CISSP exam this summer. What is their status?

Question 3 options
Correct: A. Five years are required and a degree waives only one, so they have four of five. Passing makes them an Associate of ISC2, with six years to earn the experience. Anyone may sit the exam.

Q4.A security analyst finds that a production server is missing a critical patch. What should the analyst do FIRST?

Question 4 options
Correct: C. The owner decides how to treat the risk, and changes go through change management. Patching or shutting down a production system on your own can cause an outage the business did not approve.

Q5.A candidate has two months to study. Their work is mostly network engineering, and they have never worked in software development. Given the exam’s domain weights, which plan is BEST?

Question 5 options
Correct: C. The weights run from 11.2% to 13.6%, so every domain matters and none can be skipped. The best use of time is to close gaps. Software Development Security still supplies more than one question in ten.

Q6.Why do most candidates feel they are missing many questions on an adaptive exam?

Question 6 options
Correct: A. Adaptive selection targets items near your current estimate, so everyone answers a substantial share incorrectly. The percent correct is not your score.

Q7.How many CPE credits must a CISSP earn to maintain the credential?

Question 7 options
Correct: D. CISSP requires 120 CPEs per three-year cycle, plus the annual maintenance fee. Earning about 40 a year keeps you on track.

Q8.During a ransomware response in the data center, the fire alarm sounds. What should the incident lead do FIRST?

Question 8 options
Correct: B. Human safety always comes first. Systems and evidence can be recovered or rebuilt; people cannot.
