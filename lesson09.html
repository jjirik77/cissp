Day 5, Afternoon • All Domains

Capstone: Exam Strategy and Faculty PD

Eight domains studied one at a time become one exam answered all at once. This capstone pulls the week together: how a single scenario crosses domains, how to read questions under pressure, how to plan the final weeks and exam day, and how to carry CISSP into the courses you teach and the Knowledge Units your program documents.

Session: Day 5, 1:00 to 5:00 Duration: ~3.5 hrs Exam Weight: All domains Knowledge Check: 8 Questions

Overview

From eight domains to one decision

The exam rarely labels a question by domain. A scenario about a ransomware attack can test business impact analysis, backup design, network segmentation, privileged accounts, and incident response in five consecutive items. Candidates who studied in silos see five unrelated topics. Candidates who studied the system see one organization with one set of priorities.

This lesson has two halves. The first prepares you for the exam: synthesis, tactics, the final weeks, and exam day. The second prepares you to teach: mapping the domains to your courses and to the CAE Cyber Defense Knowledge Units, and leaving with a 90-day plan.

🧭 Think like a managerIn a cross-domain scenario, ask what the organization needs most right now. The domain that answers that question usually holds the best answer.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to…

  • Analyze a single scenario for the decisions it raises in several domains. Bloom: Analyze
  • Apply question tactics to BEST, FIRST, and MOST items. Bloom: Apply
  • Evaluate your own readiness by domain, weighted by exam weight. Bloom: Evaluate
  • Map the eight domains to your courses and to CAE-CD Knowledge Units. Bloom: Apply
  • Design a 90-day plan that covers exam study and curriculum work. Bloom: Create

One Scenario, Many Domains

Consider one event. A community college is hit by ransomware on the first day of registration. Every domain has something to say about it.

DomainThe question it raises
1. Governance and RiskDid the BIA set an RTO for registration, and did leadership accept the residual risk?
2. Asset SecurityWhich data was encrypted or stolen, how was it classified, and who owns it?
3. ArchitectureWere backups isolated from production by design, and was the attacker’s zero-day entry point in the threat model?
4. Network SecurityDid segmentation contain the spread, or could it move east and west freely?
5. IAMWhich privileged account did the attacker use, and would phishing-resistant passwordless sign-in or risk-adaptable access control have stopped it?
6. AssessmentHad anyone tested a full restore against the RTO?
7. OperationsDid EDR and SOAR contain the spread, were backups immutable, and did the team preserve evidence and notify the right parties?
8. SoftwareWas the entry point a vulnerable open source component that an SBOM and software composition analysis would have flagged?

The exam will not ask all eight questions. It will ask one, and the scenario will contain details that belong to the other seven. Your task is to find the question being asked and ignore the rest.

Question Tactics

  • Read the last line first. It tells you what is asked and often who is asking. Then read the scenario for the facts that matter.
  • Find the role. A question asked of a CISO wants a governance answer. A question asked of an analyst may want a procedural one.
  • Mark the qualifier. BEST and MOST want the widest benefit. FIRST wants the earliest sound step. PRIMARY wants the main purpose.
  • Eliminate the technical fix. An option that changes a system without assessment, approval, or process is usually a distractor.
  • Watch for absolutes. Options with “always,” “never,” or “guarantees” are rarely correct in a risk-based field.
  • Choose between the last two. When two options remain, pick the one that addresses the cause, protects people, or follows governance.
Technician answer“Block the IP address at the firewall.” Fast, specific, and it treats one symptom.
Leader answer“Follow the incident response plan to contain and assess the scope.” Slower to say, and it covers the whole problem.
💡 Exam TipYou cannot return to an item on the adaptive exam, so answer each one completely before moving on. Do not guess quickly with a plan to come back. There is no coming back.

The Final Weeks and Exam Day

The final two weeks

  • Weeks before: take a full-length timed practice exam, then spend most of your remaining time on the two weakest high-weight domains.
  • Every day: fifteen minutes of flashcards from all domains. Retrieval beats rereading.
  • The last three days: review notes and the mindset filters. Stop learning new material; consolidate what you know.
  • The night before: sleep matters more than one more chapter.

Exam day

  • Arrive early with two forms of identification that match your registration exactly.
  • Expect a secure test center check-in, a locker for belongings, and possibly a palm vein or photo scan.
  • Pace at about 70 seconds per item and take an optional break if you need it; the clock keeps running.
  • You receive a provisional result at the center. An official result follows from ISC2.

After you pass

Submit the endorsement application within nine months. An active ISC2 credential holder attests to your experience, or ISC2 acts as endorser. Then start the CPE cycle: 120 credits over three years, about 40 a year, plus the annual maintenance fee. Verify current requirements at isc2.org.

Topics to review twice

The current outline added topics that many older study guides cover lightly or not at all. Give them a second pass in the final weeks:

  • Artificial intelligence: ethics in AI (Domain 1), AI threats such as adversarial AI, model poisoning, and deepfakes (Domain 3), and ML and AI based security tools (Domain 7).
  • Emerging technology: post-quantum cryptography, quantum computing, OT and ICS, and autonomous systems (Domain 3), and CNAPP (Domain 2).
  • Identity: passwordless authentication with FIDO, WebAuthn, and passkeys, and risk-adaptable access control (Domain 5).
  • Operations: SOAR, EDR and XDR, immutable and encrypted backups, and MTTR and MTBF metrics (Domain 7).
  • Software: SSDF and SBOM requirements, shift left, and memory-safe languages (Domain 8), plus software-defined security (Domain 4).

    WindowExam studyTeaching and curriculum
    Days 1 to 30Use the readiness radar. Study the two weakest high-weight domains. Five hours a week, flashcards daily.Pick one course and map it to domains and KUs. Choose one lab or activity to adopt.
    Days 31 to 60Rotate through every domain. Take one timed practice exam and log every miss by domain.Pilot the lab or activity with students. Collect the printed reports as evidence.
    Days 61 to 90Second timed practice exam. Close remaining gaps. Schedule the exam.Update the course crosswalk. Share results with your program lead or CAE point of contact.

    Faculty Teaching Segment

    The capstone pattern works in any program: one rich scenario, several lenses, and a short written defense.

    • Signature assignment: give student teams the ransomware scenario from Section 1. Each team takes two domains, answers the questions those domains raise, and presents a joint recommendation to a mock leadership panel.
    • Rubric criteria: accurate use of domain concepts, links between domains, a prioritized recommendation, and communication suited to executives.
    • Activity idea: students use the KU mapper on your own program and identify one gap. They propose a module, lab, or eMate that would fill it.
    • Course fit: a capstone or seminar course. It supports the Cybersecurity Planning and Management, Security Program Management, and Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery KUs, and the cross-domain tabletop in Lab B7.
    🎓 Teaching noteEnd the workshop as you would end a course: each participant states one exam date target and one change to a course this term. Saying it aloud makes it real.

    Scored Knowledge Check

    Test your understanding

    Select the best answer for each question, then submit for your score and the rationale for every item. Like the exam, several options may be true; choose the best one.

    Q1.After a ransomware attack, a college took four days to restore registration, although the BIA set a 24-hour RTO. Which gap MOST likely caused the miss?

    Question 1 options
    Correct: C. The question is about recovery time, not prevention. Only a tested restore shows whether the RTO can be met. The other gaps may explain how the attack happened, not why recovery was slow.

    Q2.A college plans to use an AI model to flag students at risk of dropping out, trained on advising notes and grades. Which step should the CISO recommend FIRST?

    Question 2 options
    Correct: A. This touches ethics in AI (Domain 1) and data ownership and privacy (Domain 2). Governance comes first: a lawful basis, an accountable owner, and a check that the model is fair and explainable. The other options are controls or tools chosen before the question of whether the use is appropriate.

    Q3.Two options on a question would both reduce the risk described. One reconfigures a server; the other updates the policy and assigns an owner. Which approach usually leads to the BEST answer?

    Question 3 options
    Correct: D. The leader’s answer fixes why the risk exists and assigns accountability. A single server change treats one instance of the problem.

    Q4.A lab has students hash files, encrypt data with AES, and verify a digital signature. Which CAE-CD Knowledge Unit does it MOST directly support?

    Question 4 options
    Correct: B. Hashing, symmetric encryption, and signatures are the core of Basic Cryptography. Advanced Cryptography (ACR) would add topics such as protocol design and cryptanalysis.

    Q5.Which three Knowledge Units are foundational for every CAE-CD program?

    Question 5 options
    Correct: A. The current KUs name IT Systems Components (ISC), Cybersecurity Fundamentals (CSF), and Cybersecurity Principles (CSP) as foundational. The others belong to the technical core, non-technical core, or optional KUs.

    Q6.Ransomware encrypted a college’s servers and its online backup volume. The incident review asks which single change would BEST ensure recovery next time. Which change addresses the root cause?

    Question 6 options
    Correct: C. The backups failed because the attacker could reach and encrypt them. Immutable or offline copies (Domain 7) remove that path, and restore testing (Domain 6) proves they work. More frequent or larger backups on a reachable system fail the same way, and an alert alone does not restore anything.

    Q7.Which exam-day habit fits the adaptive format BEST?

    Question 7 options
    Correct: B. You cannot skip or return to items, so each answer is final. Pace at about 70 seconds per item and commit.

    Q8.A faculty member passes the exam and has the required experience. What must they do to become certified?

    Question 8 options
    Correct: D. Endorsement confirms the experience and agreement to the Code of Ethics. CPEs begin after certification, not before.
