CISSP Security Lab

Eight hands-on labs, one per domain, built on live browser tools. Take the ticket, work the problem in the workbench, verify each checklist item, and print a graded lab report. Every lab lists the NSA CAE Cyber Defense Knowledge Units it supports, so faculty can drop it into a CAE-aligned course.

Labs: 8Each: 60 to 75 minDomains: All eightMode: Individual or teamKUs: Mapped

How the lab works

Do the work with interactive tools

Take the ticket

Each lab opens with a request from a fictional organization and a 100-point checklist.

Use the tools

Threat model board, data inventory, real Web Crypto, firewall rule tester, identity console, results analyzer, incident tabletop, and pipeline review. All run in the browser.

Verify and debrief

Check each item as often as needed. Reveal the leader’s debrief after a first try, at the cost of that item’s points.

Lab report

Print or save a graded report with decisions, written deliverables, team members, the Knowledge Units covered, and a signature line.

For faculty

Using the Security Lab in class

  • As submissions: ask learners or teams to print the lab report to PDF and upload it. Reports show the score out of 100, attempts per item, recorded answers, the written deliverable, and which debriefs were revealed.
  • Team mode: one learner drives the tool while the team decides, then the team submits one report listing every member.
  • Lab B7 tabletop: open the lab on the projector and choose Facilitator. Teams choose Team and enter the session code. When you pause or release an inject early, a new code appears; teams re-enter it to stay in sync.
  • Knowledge Units: each lab card and lab report names the 2024 CAE Cyber Defense KUs it supports. Cite the lab in your KU mapping as a hands-on activity.
  • Offline friendly: every lab runs locally in the browser, with no installs or accounts.
